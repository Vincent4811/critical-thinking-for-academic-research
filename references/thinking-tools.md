# Academic adaptations of selected thinking tools

The course's five habits are the primary process. These mechanisms are adapted from the pinned [upstream catalog](../UPSTREAM.json), with retained terms in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). They are plain local guidance, not dependencies or claims of proven academic benefit.

Select none or one tool by default. Add up to two more only when each answers a different unresolved question; name that question. Do not use the tool catalog as an obligatory checklist. Drop a frame that does not change the analysis.

| Tool | Trigger in research | Useful move | Academic boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Socratic | A concept or premise is consequentially undefined | Ask the single question whose answer most changes the analysis | Resolve accessible facts yourself; one question per learner turn, not a full interrogation |
| First Principles | A convention or assumed constraint blocks a research question | Separate established constraints, measured facts, and conventions; rebuild a feasible question | Biology is context-dependent; label uncertain premises A, retain ethics, measurement and resource constraints |
| Map–Territory | Summary, slide, figure, or author claim seems inconsistent with primary material | Compare the representation with accessible original methods, tables or data and record the mismatch | A published table is still a report; inspecting it does not validate raw data or establish causality |
| Scientific Method | Several explanations fit the observation | State distinguishing predictions and the next informative observation | Upstream targets agent-now software diagnosis and excludes multi-week experiments. This adaptation plans research discriminators; it does not import debugging localization, claim a complete scientific method, or authorize running studies |
| Steel-Manning | A preferred interpretation is accepted too easily or a credible rival dismissed | Present the strongest faithful rival and the evidence that would change the judgment | Do not invent a defense of factual errors, fabricated findings, or a retracted effect |
| Probabilistic | New evidence should change an uncertain belief or a reference class informs a forecast | State the base-rate source, uncertainty and update assumptions | Numerical priors/LRs/intervals require a justified model or data. Without them, state a qualitative conditional update; p-values are not posterior probabilities |
| Second-Order | Incentives, cooperation, selection or delayed effects matter | Map who reacts, under what incentives, and which consequences change the research decision | Use mechanism-supported conditional paths, not asserted motives or a fabricated game payoff matrix |
| Pre-Mortem | A proposed study has consequential feasibility risks | Identify a concrete failure scenario, a preventive design change, and a check | Scientific/ethical requirements are distinguished from user-approval gates; do not add ritual approvals to reversible drafting |

The router adaptation retains its main constraint: mechanism fit, with no forced composition. Numeric selection scores are not imported as a validated method-selection metric. The catalog's eval results are not evidence of efficacy in this course or across academic research.

## Transfer from the games

- Rule-sensitive strategy → define the research target and scope before generalizing.
- Guessing others' guesses → surface assumptions about participants, peers, reviewers and incentives.
- Cooperation dilemma → examine incentive conditions as a conceptual lens, without inferring dishonest intent.
- Highest bid/most attractive result → consider selection, optimism and Winner's Curse; a loss or bias is possible, not inevitable.

Use [course activities](course-activities.md) for teaching prompts. For a routine, clearly bounded evidence question, the five habits alone are sufficient.
