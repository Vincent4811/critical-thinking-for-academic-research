# Keep judgment visible when working with AI

Read this for AI-assisted research, prompt appraisal, teaching with AI, or checking a public claim. It operationalizes selected ideas from the **Thinking Critically with AI** teaching pack, with explicit corrections and attribution in [the source map](source-map.md). It is tool-neutral; no Consensus account or connector is required. The pack is teaching guidance, not a peer-reviewed evaluation of this skill.

## Match the support to the work

In **analyze**, complete useful analysis from the supplied material now. Offer a provisional interpretation with limits when sources are incomplete; do not make the user complete a reflection form or read every paper before receiving help.

In **coach**, the practice itself is part of the learning goal. Preserve the learner's first question and reason before supplying an answer. Ask one purposeful question, wait, then respond to their contribution. A short explanation without viewing the AI output can later reveal which part needs rereading; request it on its own turn. Do not demand a full checklist alongside that question.

In **design-lesson**, plan the whole sequence, including initial reasoning, source inspection, peer challenge, explanation without AI, and an owned revision. Keep future student answers blank. Use [the AI judgment lab](../teaching/ai-judgment-lab.en.md) and its Chinese counterpart when these are the learning goals.

An assistant may propose interpretations and arguments in analysis mode. Label them as proposals for the researcher to inspect; do not falsely attribute an AI-written interpretation or a decision to a learner. Protect substantive engagement without prohibiting all AI assistance.

## Inspect the question before searching

Identify a presupposed result (for example, asking *why* an unestablished benefit occurs), vague constructs, an unjustified causal interpretation, missing comparison/context, or two outcomes that need separate questions. Preserve the original and explain any edit. Start with a broad field map for a novice who lacks enough context, then narrow with inspected sources; do not assume a focused question can be invented without field knowledge.

An answerable directional hypothesis is not automatically a biased question. A randomized comparison can legitimately ask about an intervention's effect. A yes/no question is also not inherently invalid. Preserve a valid question when its answer could be beneficial, null, harmful, or uncertain; do not replace a causal target with a generic association merely to remove the word *effect*.

For a literature-discovery task, a useful contrast is a scoped neutral query and a query seeking contradictory, null, boundary-condition, or alternative findings. Keep population, outcome and other key constraints comparable. Note any scope change made by the search tool. Record the tool/database, date, actual query, filters, and inspected sources when a reproducible search is requested. Do not claim that neutral wording guarantees unbiased, exhaustive or identical results.

Choose one suitable [question framework](question-frameworks.md) only if it clarifies the question. A framework guides formulation, not study quality or causal identification. Do not mechanically turn every component into a restrictive search term.

## Separate a discovery lead from checked support

An AI summary or citation lead is not independent empirical support for its subject-matter claim. Open the underlying source, verify its identity and the material passage, and record design, population, outcome, limitation and access level. If only the abstract is accessible, give an abstract-bounded answer and leave missing methods/results U. Do not claim full-text inspection, discard all useful work, or demand an unavailable source as a universal gate.

Search-capable language models and retrieval-grounded tools can both help discovery and can still retrieve irrelevant sources, misread them, or produce unsupported links. Distinguish capabilities by the actual tool and traceability, not by a brand guarantee. If AI outputs are themselves the research object, documented outputs can be observations about that system; that does not validate their claims about the world.

When views conflict, map the claims, exact supporting passages, relevant design/context differences, shared datasets and remaining uncertainty. Seek contrary evidence without giving a weak contrary claim equal weight merely for balance. A citation graph shows relationships, not evidential independence or reliability.

## Appraise a body of evidence, not a vote

For a claim supported by a single striking paper or a retrieval meter, inspect the surrounding evidence proportionately. Check whether reports reuse participants or data, what was selected or missed, risk of bias, applicability, effect estimates and precision, and whether outcomes actually match. Ten papers are not necessarily ten independent studies. Study counts, citation counts, journal prestige and a meter's support percentage are not pooled effect estimates or certainty scores.

A relevant systematic review can help, but its methods, included evidence and applicability still need appraisal. Do not pool incompatible studies or call a narrative comparison a meta-analysis. Choose evidence appropriate to the question; no single design hierarchy answers all causal, qualitative, prevalence, diagnostic and mechanistic questions. Failure to retrieve studies is not proof of absence or a confirmed gap. Do not broaden a clinically meaningful question merely to make a meter appear.

## Demonstrate understanding and record decisions

For learning, ask the learner to put the AI output aside and explain: what the source supports, one limit, and what could change their judgment. They can speak, write, draw or use suitable language/accessibility support. Compare the content with the inspected passage, not confidence, speed, eloquence or agreement. A fluent explanation does not prove correctness; difficulty signals a place to revisit, not a diagnosis of ability or misconduct.

Record the AI contribution, the person's actual Accepted / Partly accepted / Not accepted / Need verification decision, their reason, checked source and next check. These are process records, not evidence of intellectual ownership merely because a box was checked. [The AI research worksheet](../templates/ai-research-check.en.md) and Chinese counterpart are optional scaffolds; do not require every field for a narrow request.

## Method and data boundaries

For systematic reviews, discovery is not a substitute for the requested protocol, reproducible retrieval and deduplication, fit-for-purpose validation of AI-assisted screening/extraction/appraisal, and accountable human review. Document where AI was used and its limitations in the methods when applicable; use current field/journal guidance for a real submission. PRISMA is reporting guidance, and PROSPERO eligibility is not universal. This reference does not authorize beginning a full review or claim all AI risk-of-bias assistance is impossible.

For qualitative work, retain direct engagement with participant language, context, reflexive notes and contrary cases. Any permitted AI coding suggestion remains a proposal to inspect against the data and chosen methodology, not an automatically discovered theme. Do not send participant material to a new service without the necessary authorization and applicable institutional/ethics conditions. Removing names alone does not ensure de-identification. Use fictional or appropriately authorized material for public teaching examples. Apply these checks to the affected data operation, not as a reason to halt unrelated conceptual work.
