# Teacher activity cards

[繁體中文](activity-cards.zh-TW.md) · Give learners the [worksheet](../templates/classroom-worksheet.en.md); reserve worked debriefs until after their first attempt. These are executable teaching designs, not evidence of learning effectiveness. Use symbolic points, not money or consequential rewards.

## 1. The Last Token — Define and Challenge

**Target:** identify which rules and starting conditions a strategy depends on. **Time:** 12 minutes. **Materials/roles:** 12 tokens per pair, author/player A, player B; an optional observer records moves rather than deciding for players.

**Rules:** two players alternate; remove one or two remaining tokens, never more than remain. The player removing the last token wins. No passing. Start with 12; later change only the starting number to 13.

**Sequence:** explain rules (2 min); write a first strategy/reason and play (3); choose first/second with a written reason and replay (2); change the start to 13 and predict before playing (2); debrief and research transfer (3).

**Teacher key:** under best play, multiples of three are losing positions for the player about to move. At 12, the second player can respond so two turns remove three. At 13, the first player can take one, then use the same response pattern. Opponent mistakes can change actual outcomes; a single win does not establish the strategy.

**Debrief:** What premise changed? Could one win establish a general rule? Research transfer: replace “muscle function” with a specified construct/measure and state the conditions under which the comparison answers it.

**Individual product:** first strategy and reason; rule-dependent revision/retention; one research term that needs defining. Grade the reason and scope, not the win.

## 2. Guess the Crowd — Challenge and Revise

**Target:** separate correct arithmetic from a behavioral assumption. **Time:** 12 minutes. **Materials:** slips or an offline table; each learner chooses privately, teacher aggregates the **whole class**.

**Rules:** choose a number from 0 to 100, decimals allowed. Target = two-thirds of the mean of all submitted choices, including one's own. Minimum absolute distance wins; equal distances share the win. No choice is changed after seeing the target.

**Sequence:** instructions (2 min); private expected class average, choice and reason (2); reveal choices and calculate target (2); discuss one assumption and research transfer (3); second private choice, reveal and revision reason (3).

**Arithmetic-only illustration:** fictional choices 30, 45, 60 give mean 45 and target 30. This illustrates calculation, not actual class behavior or an equilibrium claim.

**Debrief:** What did your choice assume about others? Was your arithmetic wrong, your expectation unsupported, or both? What changed between rounds? Link to assumptions about participant behavior, adherence or peer judgment without inferring motives from one choice.

**Individual product:** both expected averages/choices, original reason, update/retention reason, and one evidence item that would change a research assumption. Winning is not a grade.

## 3. Winner's Curse — Evidence and Alternatives

**Target:** distinguish winning from a justified estimate and consider selection. **Time:** 12 minutes. **Materials:** a visible sealed jar of tokens; teacher records its actual count before class. Learners see the same jar without opening it.

**Rules:** privately record an estimate, a non-negative integer bid and a reason. Highest bid wins; ties are resolved by a random draw. Winner's symbolic score = actual count − winning bid; everyone else scores zero. A negative score is allowed. No real transaction occurs.

**Sequence:** explain and inspect jar (2 min); private estimate/bid/reason (2); collect bids and select winner (2); reveal the count and calculate score (2); debrief and research transfer (4).

**Fictional illustration:** actual count 60, highest bid 75 → winner scores −15. This does not imply every winner must lose. Risk depends on estimates, bids and the selection rule.

**Debrief:** What did you observe versus estimate? Did the rule select a high estimate? Could a “best-looking” effect, novelty claim or feasibility estimate be high partly because it was selected? What independent evidence would check it?

**Individual product:** estimate/bid, source of uncertainty, score, and one condition under which a favored research idea could be overestimated. Avoid diagnosing overconfidence as a personality trait.

## 4. Prisoner's Dilemma — new extension: incentives and second-order reasoning

**Provenance:** a new protocol designed for this package. The supplied slides contained the conceptual connection, not this matrix. **Target:** distinguish individual incentives, joint outcomes and assumptions about others. **Time:** 15 minutes. **Materials/roles:** pairs, private C/D slips and score records.

**Rules:** simultaneous private choices C (cooperate) or D (defect). Reveal together. Assign symbolic points using the matrix, with entries **(A's points, B's points)**:

| A chooses / B chooses | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | (3, 3) | (0, 5) |
| D | (5, 0) | (1, 1) |

**Sequence:** explain matrix (2 min); predict partner choice, choose and write a reason (2); reveal/score the one-shot round (1); announce and play four further rounds with the same partner, revealing each round (4); privately reflect (2); discuss assumptions and research transfer (4).

**Teacher key:** for the stated one-shot points, choosing D gives more individual points against either partner choice, while (C,C) gives more combined points than (D,D). These conclusions depend on the defined game and self-regarding point payoff; they do not establish people's motives or what an ethical action is. A fixed repeated sequence does not guarantee cooperation, and a few classroom choices are not a validated behavioral experiment.

**Debrief:** Which incentives did the matrix create? What changed when interaction was repeated? In research, what incentives could influence reporting or collaboration, and what observed evidence would be needed before inferring that they actually did? Do not equate a D choice with dishonesty or penalize it morally.

**Individual product:** initial prediction/reason, observed choices, a conditional explanation, and one design/transparency measure that could align an incentive with a research objective. Evaluate reasoning rather than cooperation.

## 5. Claim–evidence lab — Evidence, Alternatives and Revise

**Target:** trace a numerical/causal claim and state a justified limit. **Time:** 15 minutes. **Materials:** the [serving-bowl appraisal](../examples/serving-bowl.en.md) and its source locators, or a clearly fictional packet. For the historical packet explicitly mark **reported values from a retracted article; not validated effects**.

**Sequence:** identify packet/source status (2 min); individually write a judgment and reason about “smaller bowls make everyone eat less” (2); inspect Methods/table information and mark E/A/U (4); peers each ask one source-linked challenge in turn (3); learner revises/retains with reason (2); choose one research check (2).

**Source questions:** Was allocation random or alternating? Which 40/35-person target is being described? Does a displayed median support a mean claim? What else changed with capacity? What does retraction change, and what does it not show? Select relevant questions rather than firing all at the learner at once.

**Debrief key:** see the worked appraisal; no participant data or confidence interval is reconstructed. A hypothetical sensitivity assumption is not observed intake. If status is revealed in stages, call it a teaching simulation and always finish with the status and its consequence visible.

**Individual product:** one traceable E-report/status entry, one unsupported A or U, one plausible rival and discriminator, and a bounded final judgment. A correctly limited retention is acceptable.

## 6. Question-map clinic — transfer to the learner's own work

**Target:** use evidence to sharpen a personal research question. **Time:** 15 minutes individual mapping plus 18 minutes peer clinic in the default lesson. **Materials:** personal question/reason, one source when available, [map](../templates/mindmap.en.md), [coaching record](../templates/coach-session.en.md).

Map five branches with E/A/U and one labeled relationship. In triads rotate author/questioner/recorder through three 6-minute turns; in pairs use two 9-minute turns. Each author receives clarification, evidence and alternative/discriminator prompts, one at a time. Refer to a node/source rather than judging the whole idea as “wrong.”

Finish with the learner's Accepted / Partly accepted / Not accepted / Need verification decision, reason, and next evidence. Preserve the first map. Actual learner answers remain blank until supplied. Use the [rubric](../evals/rubric.md) for formative feedback, not a validated ability score.
