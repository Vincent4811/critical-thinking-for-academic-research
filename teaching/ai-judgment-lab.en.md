# Could you explain it after closing the AI window?

[繁體中文](ai-judgment-lab.zh-TW.md) · A newly designed 50-minute AI judgment lab, informed by the attributed [teaching pack](../references/source-map.md). This is a runnable proposal, not a tested class or learning-effect claim.

## Preparation and target

Default: research learners working in pairs or triads. Everyone records an individual first reason and final decision. For nine learners use three triads; run groups simultaneously. The target is to connect a claim to what a source actually supports, notice retrieval/prompt limits, and explain a justified update or retention.

Materials: [AI research worksheet](../templates/ai-research-check.en.md), pen/paper, timer, the two fictional packets below, and optionally one lawful accessible abstract/full-text excerpt selected by the instructor. No online account is needed. A live tool can replace the simulated outputs within the same time budget; record actual queries/access and do not promise the same results.

**Fictional prompt pair:** A asks, “Find proof that fermented soy drink improves student fatigue.” B asks, “What findings support, challenge, or leave uncertain the effect of a defined fermented soy drink versus a comparator on a defined fatigue outcome in students?” The drink, comparator and outcome still require definition. These are teaching prompts, not clinical advice.

**Packet 1 — simulated discovery display, not research evidence:** ten fictional search cards appear to favor benefit. Eight cards have dataset ID D1 (repeated reports); two have D2. Their effect estimates, methods, precision, and publication-selection process are absent. A decorative display says “10/10 support.” Ask what can actually be inferred. The data allow checking 10 reports versus 2 identified datasets, not effectiveness or certainty. Even D1 and D2 being fully independent remains unverified. Do not invent studies or scientific citations for these cards.

**Packet 2 — invented source excerpt for reasoning practice only:** “In a survey of 60 volunteers, beverage use and self-reported fatigue were collected at the same visit. Beverage users reported lower fatigue scores. Allocation was not randomized, and baseline fatigue and sleep were not recorded.” A simulated AI sentence adds: “The beverage therefore improves fatigue.” These sentences describe no actual study. The causal extension is unsupported by the excerpt; selection, sleep or reverse direction remain candidates, not established explanations. Replace this packet with a real source when actual literature appraisal is the objective.

## Agenda: exactly 50 minutes

| Minutes | Activity | Teacher action | Individual output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Own the first judgment | Show the claim without the AI display; ask for an initial reason | First judgment, reason, one unknown |
| 5–13 | Prompt comparison | Two minutes to explain rules, three to inspect/reframe, three to compare with a peer; preserve the inferential target | Original/revised or retained prompt with reason |
| 13–25 | Source audit | Reveal both packets; six minutes to inspect, four to compare claim and passage, two to identify access limits | Source locator, unsupported extension, 10-report/2-dataset distinction |
| 25–35 | Challenge and map | Ask groups to map missing/contrary evidence and one discriminator; give everyone a turn | Each learner's E/A/U entries and next check |
| 35–43 | Explain without AI | Two minutes of instructions; pairs have three minutes per learner, triads two minutes per learner; listener checks content against packet | Individual explanation plus a source-based correction or justified retention |
| 43–50 | Revise and exit | Four minutes for owned decision and log; three for exit ticket and collection | Actual feedback decision, reason and next evidence |

## Teacher prompts and assessment

- “What would make this question answerable in a direction you did not expect?”
- “Which exact passage supports the AI sentence, and which part did the AI add?”
- “What is being counted: reports, datasets, participants, or effect estimates?”
- “What could change your view? What can you explain without the display?”

For actual learner coaching, ask one of these at a time and wait. During preparation the teacher can plan them together.

Assess the link to the source, limitation, alternative and revision reason with [the existing rubric](../evals/rubric.md). Permit written, spoken, drawn or otherwise accessible responses; do not grade language fluency, confidence, compliance or an effect estimate not supplied. Fluent recall is not proof of understanding. Learners may keep or withhold their original judgment for a reason. Treat content uncertainty as a rereading target, not evidence of cheating or low ability. Leave actual learner answers unfilled in prepared worksheets.

Exit ticket: “The AI suggested ____. I checked ____. I now keep/change/withhold ____ because ____. My next check is ____.” With the fictional packet, students must not claim they verified a real health effect.

## A 15-minute replacement inside the existing workshop

Replace the 44–59 claim–evidence lab in [the 120-minute workshop](lesson-120.en.md); do not add another block. Use two minutes for prompt comparison, five for one selected packet/source, four for paired explanation (two per learner; in triads use one minute each plus one minute of feedback), and four for an individual decision/log. All other agenda rows stay unchanged, so the total remains 120 minutes. This is a shortened option, not all six stages of the 50-minute lab squeezed into 15 minutes.
