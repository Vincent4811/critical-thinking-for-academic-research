# Critical Thinking for Academic Research: 120-minute workshop

[繁體中文](lesson-120.zh-TW.md) · Teacher-facing worked design, not a record of a completed class.

## Audience, objectives and preparation

Default: graduate research learners, working simultaneously in triads or pairs. Adapt to the stated class size. By the end, each learner should be able to:

1. Identify an assumption in an initial research judgment.
2. Define an answerable uncertainty and distinguish the construct from its measure.
3. Trace a material claim to a source and identify a limitation or alternative.
4. Explain a revision or justified retention and choose the next discriminating evidence.

Bring one tentative research question and one paper/excerpt with a recoverable locator, when available. Without papers, use the [fictional nutrition case](../examples/nutrition.en.md); mark missing empirical evidence U. Teacher materials: [activity cards](activity-cards.en.md), tokens, paper/pens, a jar with a teacher-recorded count, calculator/spreadsheet if desired, [learner worksheet](../templates/classroom-worksheet.en.md), [MindMap template](../templates/mindmap.en.md), and [rubric](../evals/rubric.md). No online account is required.

## Agenda: exactly 120 minutes

| Minutes | Activity | Teacher move | Individual evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 | First judgment | Ask for the question, first explanation and reason; do not supply a revised question | Original wording and reason |
| 8–20 | The Last Token | Run card 1, including the 12-to-13 rule-state change and debrief | Initial strategy, assumption, revised scope |
| 20–32 | Guess the Crowd | Run card 2 with two rounds and a research transfer | Expected average, choice, reason for update/retention |
| 32–44 | Winner's Curse | Run card 3; distinguish winning from a justified estimate | Estimate, bid, symbolic outcome, selection-bias reflection |
| 44–59 | Claim–evidence lab | Run card 5 with a source packet; keep reporting/status/validity distinct | One traceable E entry, one A/U, one alternative |
| 59–74 | Individual question map | Model only the map structure, then let learners map their own question | Five-branch map, E/A/U and one labeled relation |
| 74–92 | Peer Research Question Clinic | Triads: three 6-minute author rotations; pairs: two 9-minute rotations | Evidence-based feedback tied to a specific map node |
| 92–107 | Individual revision | Learners decide which feedback to accept, reject or verify | Original/final question, decision, reason, next evidence |
| 107–116 | Share and synthesize | Three volunteers at two minutes each, then three minutes of synthesis; others submit their records | Short explanation of reasoning change or retention |
| 116–120 | Exit ticket | Collect a question, strongest limit and next check | Actionable next-evidence statement |

The total includes instructions, scoring and debriefs. Do not add the optional game without adjusting time.

## Peer-clinic instructions

Each triad rotation lasts six minutes: author explains (1), question clarification (1), source/evidence check (1), alternative/discriminator question (1), author responds and chooses a feedback status (1), recorder confirms the next check (1). Rotate author/questioner/recorder so every learner owns a turn. In pairs, allow nine minutes per author for the same sequence.

Point to a node or source locator. Ask **one question at a time** and listen before following up. Useful English stems:

- “When you say ____, what would you actually observe?”
- “Which part of the source supports ____?”
- “Could ____ also explain it?”
- “What observation would distinguish ____ from ____?”

Do not rewrite the author's topic for them. They may keep it with a reason. For a large class, keep group work simultaneous; the nine-minute share block is selected examples, not a promise that everyone will present.

## Teacher debrief and assessment

Connect each game to a specific research habit, then apply the same habit to the source/map. Do not grade by game score, cooperation or changing one's answer. Use the four existing rubric dimensions for formative feedback: question clarity, evidence fit, other explanations, revision reason/next step. Keep the original artifact so the comparison is inspectable; do not claim a validated ability change.

Exit ticket: “My question is ____. The inspected source supports ____, but not ____. My next check is ____ because its result would change ____.” A learner with no verified source should say so rather than fill the blanks with invented evidence.

## Optional Prisoner's Dilemma extension

Card 4 supplies a newly designed 15-minute protocol. To keep **120 minutes**, replace the 12-minute Winner's Curse block with this 15-minute activity and reduce sharing/synthesis from nine to six minutes. Keep all other blocks unchanged. To run all four games with all original blocks, explicitly plan **135 minutes**. This extension is not presented as a protocol recovered from the original slides.

## 50-minute adaptation

| Minutes | Activity | Retained output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | First question and reason | Initial judgment |
| 5–15 | Guess the Crowd, shortened to one reveal and debrief | One assumption and its scope |
| 15–25 | Question map using a preselected excerpt or fictional case | E/A/U map with a key unknown |
| 25–37 | Peer check in pairs: six minutes per author | One source-linked challenge and discriminator |
| 37–46 | Individual revision | Learner-owned decision/reason |
| 46–50 | Exit ticket | Next evidence |

Avoid running all games in this shorter lesson. Keep the chain from first judgment to evidence, challenge and revision rather than reducing each activity to a demonstration without learner reasoning.
